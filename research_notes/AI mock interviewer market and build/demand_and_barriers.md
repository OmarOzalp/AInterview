# Demand, market size, and barriers for AI interview-prep tools for tech job seekers

Research limits: ~12 tool calls. WebFetch could not reach interviewing.io, malpaniventures.com, or newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com (hosts not in the session's allowed network domains), so those were seen only as search snippets. Most findings below come from search-result summaries and secondary sources. Primary pages (NY Fed, BLS, layoffs.fyi, OpenAI pricing) were NOT opened. Source quality is flagged on each line.

## 1. State of the tech job market 2025-2026 (new grad / early career / experienced; applicant volume, interview loops, AI in hiring)

### Takeaway
Entry-level tech is the weakest segment (hiring well below 2019 levels, CS grad unemployment above the all-grad average), while overall software openings reportedly rebounded in 2026 and layoffs continue. Interviews are being reshaped by AI-assisted cheating (some firms returning to in-person rounds) and by employers adopting AI interviewers, which candidates distrust.

### Cited Findings
- New-grad hiring at big US tech firms reported at under half of 2019 levels (cites a Blind article); another blog says new-grad hiring down 60% at major tech companies. Weak source (blog aggregation) — [search results incl. Blind "the broken ladder"](https://www.teamblind.com/news/the-broken-ladder)
- Claim: "entry-level software engineer" postings grew 47% (Oct 2023-Nov 2024) while actual hiring into them fell 73%. Single secondary blog, unverified — [Extern / hakia / metaintro results](https://hakia.com/news/entry-level-tech-hiring-crisis-2026/)
- TrueUp data, via an outlet, shows software engineering openings up ~30% in 2026 to 67,000+ at tech companies; same article notes 52,000 tech workers laid off — [implicator.ai](https://www.implicator.ai/software-engineering-openings-jump-30-even-as-52-000-tech-workers-lose-jobs/) (secondary)
- IBM reportedly said Feb 2026 it would triple entry-level hiring; unverified secondary blog claim — [search result](https://www.danilchenko.dev/posts/junior-developer-jobs-2026/)
- NY Fed-derived figures reported in 2026 coverage: CS majors ~6.1-7.0% unemployment, computer engineering ~7.5-8% (numbers differ by data vintage); all recent grads ~5.6-5.7% unemployment, ~42% underemployment. Secondary blogs, need confirming against NY Fed table — [f1jobs.io summary](https://www.f1jobs.io/resources/blog/computer-science-unemployment-rate-2026-international-grads)
- Counter-evidence: an NBER working paper reportedly found no summer-2026 spike in recent-grad unemployment and no significant AI-linked relative increase — same search results as above (secondary report of the paper; paper not read)
- Entry-level hiring for computer/information sciences grads reportedly fell ~24% (Forbes via an outlet) — [VnExpress](https://e.vnexpress.net/news/tech/tech-news/computer-science-graduates-see-second-largest-drop-in-us-entry-level-hiring-5095293.html)
- Layoffs.fyi 2026 count reportedly 128,536 tech employees at 299 companies Jan 1-Sep 10, 2026, already passing a quoted 2025 total of 122,606 (the same source also cites 154,445 for full-year 2025; other trackers say 245,000 under broader definitions; Crunchbase pace reportedly lower). Figures inconsistent — [Futurism](https://futurism.com/future-society/2026-tech-layoffs-surpass-2025), [attackofthefanboy](https://attackofthefanboy.com/news/tech-companies-laid-off-122606-workers-in-all-of-2025-that-number-has-already-been-surpassed-in-2026/)
- Cheating/AI in interviews: in a Pragmatic Engineer-reported interviewer survey, 81% of interviewers at Big Tech/"new Big Tech" suspected candidates of using AI to cheat and 31% said they caught one; Google piloted in-person SWE onsites (reportedly 2 virtual + 3-4 in-person rounds); Cisco, McKinsey and Deloitte UK added in-person stages; Meta planning an AI-assisted interview round; Anthropic bans AI use in interviews — [Pragmatic Engineer (snippet)](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/the-pulse-146), [Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/brand-makers/google-to-reintroduce-in-person-interviews-amid-rising-ai-cheating-concerns-79624.htm)
- "72% of companies fighting AI fraud with in-person interviews" and "in-person requests rose 5% to 30%" are from a staffing/recruiting-coach blog/single staffing firm's clients; not industry-wide — [The Interview Guys](https://blog.theinterviewguys.com/?p=8276). Weak/promotional.
- AI interviewers by employers: Greenhouse survey (1,200 US workers; vendor with a commercial interest): 63% of job seekers have faced an AI interview (+13 pts in 6 months); 38% walked away from a process because of it; 70% weren't clearly told AI was evaluating them; of those completing AI interviews, 28% advanced, 13% rejected, 51% got no response — [Greenhouse](https://www.greenhouse.com/newsroom/63-of-job-seekers-have-faced-an-ai-interview-most-havent-had-a-good-one-yet)
- Applicant volume per role: I found no reliable, citable 2025-26 applicants-per-opening figure for software roles in this pass.

### Inferences
- Higher competition + AI-in-the-loop interviews likely raises candidates' perceived need to practice, especially entry-level; the many-applicants/few-offers environment means candidates face repeated loops (more rounds = more practice demand). This is inference, not measured.
- The in-person revival and AI-interviewer adoption mean practice that mimics a live human/AI conversation (not just LeetCode) matters more.

### Gaps
- No primary NY Fed/BLS/layoffs.fyi pages opened; numbers conflict between secondary sources.
- No reliable applicants-per-job data; no data on the number of interview loops per offer.

## 2. Evidence of demand, willingness to pay, and market size

### Takeaway
Candidates already pay $99/mo up to $225+/session for human or hybrid prep, and several AI "interview copilot" companies raised money and claim ~1M users, which indicates real demand. Published market-size numbers are low-quality and wildly inconsistent, so no trustworthy TAM exists for this niche.

### Cited Findings
- interviewing.io expert mocks: $225/session in one source, $179 in another, $150 in an older one; coaching packages $1,500-$4,000. Third-party comparison sites, confirm on provider page — [Lodely](https://www.lodely.com/blog/interviewing-io-pricing), [techinterview.org](https://www.techinterview.org/post/3233474681/mock-interview-platforms/)
- Exponent: $99/mo or $299/yr per one listing — [Lodely](https://www.lodely.com/blog/exponent-pricing). Pramp: free peer mocks (sources conflict on paid AI tier) — [fitgap](https://us.fitgap.com/products/pramp). Prepfully pricing not found.
- Final Round AI: ~$6.88M seed (Jan 2025, led by Uncork Capital per one source); claims 1M+ users from 100+ countries as of July 2025 (company claim, via aggregator). Series A / ARR NOT verified — the "$16M Series A BlackRock" text on Caplight is a site banner unrelated to the company — [Seedtable](https://seedtable.com/companies/final-round-ai/funding-rounds/seed-2025-01), [Caplight](https://www.caplight.com/company/finalroundai)
- Cluely ("cheat on everything"): $5.3M seed (Abstract, Susa), later reported $15M from a16z (headline only); $3M ARR / 70,000 users claim from a single weak aggregator — [eWEEK](https://eweek.com/news/cluely-ai-cheating-app), [Techleap](https://finder.techleap.nl/news/feed/cluely-raises-15m-from-a16z)
- Free competition: ChatGPT Advanced Voice and Gemini Live are described as free/cheap mock interview tools — [fast.io roundup](https://fast.io/resources/best-ai-interview-prep-tools-2026/) (listicle, treat cautiously)
- Market-size reports (all low quality, paid-report marketing): interview preparation tool market $2.8B in 2025 -> $5.03B by 2030 (Business Research Company); $1.78B in 2025 -> $5.5B 2035 (Wise Guy); $2.5B 2023 -> $6.3B 2031 (Verified Market Research). Career coaching: $9.8B in 2024 (Credence), $13.6B in 2024 (Data Horizzon), $1.43B in 2026 (Simply Coach roundup, itself a roundup) — spread ~10x. [BRC](https://www.thebusinessresearchcompany.com/report/interview-preparation-tool-market-report), [Wise Guy](https://www.wiseguyreports.com/reports/interview-preparation-tool-market), [VMR](https://www.verifiedmarketresearch.com/product/interview-preparation-tool-market/), [Credence](https://www.credenceresearch.com/report/career-coaching-service-market), [Data Horizzon](https://datahorizzonresearch.com/career-coaching-service-market-46875)
- A VC blog titled "why we passed an opportunity to invest in the mock interview prep space" exists (Malpani Ventures); content not accessible — [link](https://www.malpaniventures.com/blog/why-we-passed-an-opportunity-to-invest-in-the-mock-interview-prep-space)

### Inferences
- A bottom-up estimate is more credible than these reports: e.g., (US tech new grads + job switchers actively interviewing per year) x (share paying for prep) x (spend). I did not have verified inputs; do this with real numbers before using.
- Price anchors suggest candidates will pay $20-100/mo for something near human-quality, but the existence of free ChatGPT/Gemini voice caps B2C pricing power for commodity "practice questions."

### Gaps
- No Google Trends / search-volume data retrieved; no actual Reddit/HN/LinkedIn thread quotes obtained (search returned only listicles and vendor pages).
- No verified revenue for any AI interview-prep company; no bootcamp pricing figures retrieved.
- Market-size numbers cannot be validated (methodology undisclosed).

## 3. Why it is not yet well solved (barriers)

### Takeaway
Evidence found supports: cheating/AI-assist backlash, candidate distrust of AI evaluation, and nontrivial voice cost, plus free general assistants. Evidence on churn/retention, evaluation reliability, realism complaints, and CAC was NOT found and remains hypothesis.

### Cited Findings
- Backlash/legitimacy: cheating tools (Cluely, Interview Coder) triggered outrage and employer countermeasures (in-person rounds, bans) — [eWEEK](https://eweek.com/news/cluely-ai-cheating-app), [Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/brand-makers/google-to-reintroduce-in-person-interviews-amid-rising-ai-cheating-concerns-79624.htm). A practice-only product risks association with the "copilot" category that the market leader Final Round AI sells (stealth/"undetectable" features) — [search profile](https://www.everydev.ai/developers/final-round-ai)
- Voice unit economics: third-party estimates for OpenAI realtime audio range from ~$0.04-0.10/min (with caching, 2.1) to ~$0.30-0.45/min (gpt-realtime-2); token prices $32/$64 per 1M audio in/out for 2.1, mini about $10/$20. Not verified against OpenAI's page. A 45-min mock = roughly $2-$20 depending on assumptions — [Forasoft](https://www.forasoft.com/blog/article/openai-realtime-api-pricing), [eesel](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/gpt-realtime-mini-pricing). The arithmetic for a 45-min session is my calculation.
- Voice realism: only a builder's opinion (Indie Hackers) that native voice-to-voice, rather than STT/TTS chaining, is what makes it feel human — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/how-i-built-mockreal-ai-mock-interviews-that-actually-feel-real-a0e6f6cdb4). No independent user complaints retrieved.
- Candidate trust/bias: candidates flag similar bias levels for AI and human interviewers (36% age, 27% race); only 21% believe most employers use AI responsibly; 59% (SHL) say AI makes bias worse; 19% want less AI, 46% want the option of a human — [Greenhouse report](https://www.greenhouse.com/uk/blog/2026-candidate-ai-interview-report), [HR Dive](https://hrdive.com/news/us-workers-report-a-major-ai-trust-gap/806151/). Vendor-run surveys; these are about employer-side AI, only indirectly about practice tools.

### Inferences
- Plausible but untested: short usage window (2-3 months) implies high B2C churn; CAC competes with SEO-optimized incumbents and free ChatGPT; evaluation reliability (LLM judging answers/system-design diagrams) is the likely quality gap. None verified here — treat as hypotheses to test with interviews.
- Voice cost is probably manageable at a per-session price (a $10-30 session vs. $2-20 cost estimate looks thin at the high end, comfortable at the low end), but flat monthly subscriptions with heavy users are risky.

### Gaps
- No churn/retention data, startup post-mortems, or CAC data found (search for these returned only generic churn material).
- No evidence on code-execution/whiteboard/system-design tooling quality of existing products.
- Malpani Ventures and interviewing.io posts could not be fetched; they might contain relevant arguments.

## 4. Customer segments (B2C, universities/career centers, bootcamps, employers)

### Takeaway
No segment-specific data was found in this pass. Reasoning below is inference only.

### Cited Findings
- Employers already adopt AI interviewers (63% of job seekers faced one; Greenhouse), showing budget exists on the employer side, but with candidate attrition (38% walked away) — [Greenhouse](https://www.greenhouse.com/newsroom/63-of-job-seekers-have-faced-an-ai-interview-most-havent-had-a-good-one-yet)
- Career-coaching report segments clients as Students, Professionals, Executives, Career Changers — [Verified Market Research](https://www.verifiedmarketresearch.com/product/career-coaching-service-market/) (promotional)

### Inferences
- B2C: fastest feedback, highest willingness to pay at moments of need, but seasonal/short LTV and high CAC (inference).
- Universities/career centers & bootcamps: recurring cohorts, outcomes-driven (placement rates), lower churn per contract but slow procurement and low price per seat (inference). Free-tier Pramp-style competition and free ChatGPT pressure price.
- Employers: bigger budgets and clearer ROI (screening), but different product (evaluation, compliance/bias law exposure) and candidate-trust risk (Greenhouse data).

### Gaps
- No pricing, sales-cycle, or contract data for university/bootcamp/employer deals; no career-center reports retrieved.
